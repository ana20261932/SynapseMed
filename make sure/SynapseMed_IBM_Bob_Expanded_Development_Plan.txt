# SynapseMed — Expanded IBM Bob Build Plan and Free-Tier Toolkit

**Purpose:** Extend SynapseMed into a South African multi-tenant medical practice management system with configurable clinical forms, a unified patient timeline, medical-aid billing, a simulated claims switch, AI-assisted document workflows, backups, and an IBM Cloud deployment path.

**Status:** This is a development specification and a coding-agent prompt, not a completed or production-ready medical application, legal opinion, production deployment, switch connection, or compliance certification.

**Assumptions:** The existing app uses Next.js/React/TypeScript, Node.js, and MongoDB. IBM Bob is the coding assistant. Adapt to the actual repository after inspecting it; do not force a rewrite.

## 1. Non-negotiable product and safety rules

1. Start with synthetic/demo records only. Do not upload identifiable patient files to IBM Bob, public AI tools, demo environments, issue trackers, logs, or unapproved storage.
2. Build a clearly labelled simulated medical-aid switch first. Never imply that a real claim has been submitted until an authorised switch integration has returned a genuine response.
3. AI output is a draft. A qualified clinician must review clinical content and an authorised billing user must review claims before any record or claim is committed or submitted.
4. Determine tenant access from the authenticated user's server-side membership and permissions. **Do not trust an `X-Tenant-ID` header by itself** as proof of tenant access.
5. Keep audit history for sensitive record reads, writes, exports, AI generation/review, access changes, invoices, and claims. Avoid writing clinical content or secrets into logs.
6. Keep PMS subscription billing separate from medical-aid switch fees. Never store raw card numbers or CVV; use a tokenised payment provider if subscriptions are added.
7. Do not describe the product as “POPIA compliant” or “HIPAA compliant” because a prompt says so. Compliance requires a proper legal, privacy, security, operational, and contractual assessment.
8. Verify IBM Cloud plan, quota, billing, service region, service-specific data location, and retention before provisioning. A tool being listed in IBM Cloud Catalog does not mean it is permanently free or appropriate for live health data.
9. Do not put encryption keys, API keys, MongoDB credentials, service credentials, or medical-aid switch credentials into Git, client bundles, test fixtures, or `.env.example` values.
10. Use AI and hosted IBM services with synthetic or appropriately de-identified content until privacy/legal review, contractual terms, service region and data-processing details have been approved for the intended patient data.

## 2. Product features to build

### A. Practice administration

- Multiple practices/tenants and branches.
- Staff accounts, provider profiles, roles, permissions, and practice settings.
- Configurable practitioner types and specialties rather than a doctor-only design.
- Patient registration, medical-aid membership details, contact details, patient search, appointments and appointment statuses.
- South African context: `countryCode = ZA`, default display timezone `Africa/Johannesburg`, configurable date/number formatting, and South African address/contact formats.

### B. Template-driven clinical encounter canvas

Build `ClinicalEncounterCanvas` using the existing Next.js App Router, TypeScript, and existing UI/component conventions (Tailwind only if already in the project or approved for addition).

- Split-screen workspace with an editable clinical note on the left and an “AI Draft Insights” panel on the right.
- Encounter templates that can be chosen by specialty/use case, e.g. general consultation, paediatrics, chronic disease review, dental, physiotherapy, psychology, and optometry. These are sample template categories, not a claim that every profession has the same rules.
- Template versioning: a saved encounter records the template version used so that older encounters remain interpretable after a template changes.
- Example form areas: Chief Complaint, History, Objective/Vitals, Examination, Assessment, Plan, Medicines/Allergies, Referrals, and Follow-up. Make sections configurable.
- Draft autosave with visible save state, safe retry, and no loss of unsaved text when another panel fails.
- An explicit `Verify and Save to Patient Timeline` action. AI draft suggestions must not silently write to a signed/final clinical record.
- Accessible keyboard navigation, loading states, validation errors, unsaved-change warning and responsive layout.

### C. Unified patient encounter timeline

Create a chronological patient feed showing permitted events such as appointments, encounters, clinical notes, uploaded documents, referrals, prescriptions, claim statuses and payment/invoice events. Each event must have a timestamp, type, source reference, tenant scope and permission check. Display timestamps for users in `Africa/Johannesburg` while storing timestamps consistently (UTC recommended).

Do not disclose a claim or billing event to clinical users who do not have permission to view it. Timeline aggregation must not bypass the normal authorisation rules of each source module.

### D. South African billing and claims

- Configurable service catalogue and versioned tariffs.
- A source-approved/current ICD-10 lookup dataset and documented data licence; do not invent or hardcode clinical coding rules.
- Invoice lines with explicit medical-aid target amount, patient co-payment, adjustments and payment allocations. Amounts must use decimal-safe money handling (integer cents or an approved decimal type), never JavaScript binary floating-point arithmetic as the sole financial representation.
- Claim lifecycle: `draft -> validated -> awaiting_approval -> queued -> simulated_submitted -> response_received -> accepted/rejected/queried -> corrected/resubmitted`.
- Idempotency keys, duplicate-claim checks, claim event history, timeout handling, reconciliation and exportable reports.
- Show clearly that all demo responses are simulated.
- Test fixtures should deterministically cover approved, rejected (e.g. synthetic invalid membership or missing/invalid code), queried/pending, duplicate, timeout and retry scenarios. Random approval percentages may be used only in a clearly labelled demo, not in unit tests or real billing logic.
- Do not assume every medical-aid scheme has the same rules or that one universal open-source switch works in South Africa.

### E. AI medical-document assistant via watsonx.ai

Provide an `AIProvider` interface, with a mock provider as the default. Add an IBM watsonx.ai provider behind a server-side adapter only after credentials, region and account access are configured.

Allowed initial workflows:
- Summarise a synthetic/de-identified uploaded document with traceable source references.
- Draft structured clinical-note sections for clinician review.
- Extract candidate fields from medical documents and show confidence/uncertainty.
- Identify missing or inconsistent claim fields as suggestions; do not decide cover, coverage or final liability.
- Suggest an ICD-10 search term/category for a human to review; do not automatically finalise a diagnosis or claim code.

Implementation controls:
- Keep all provider calls server-side. Never send keys or patient payloads from the browser directly to watsonx.ai.
- Use an allowlisted request schema and structured data minimisation. Regex-based redaction alone is not a reliable de-identification method. Detect/review direct identifiers, omit nonessential fields, and retain a safe source-to-result link locally.
- Treat uploaded text as untrusted data, not instructions. Defend against prompt injection and file-borne instructions.
- Store provider, model ID, prompt/template version, timestamp, source document references, error state, and human-review state. Do not log raw prompts or clinical text by default.
- Require human approval before committing AI-generated fields into an encounter or claim.
- Handle timeouts, rate limits, provider errors, model changes, unavailable region, and token limits. Always provide a manual workflow when AI is unavailable.
- Pin generation settings only after testing; a low temperature does not prevent hallucinations or make outputs clinically safe.
- Load actual base endpoint, API version and model ID from the current IBM developer-access page/account and supported-model list. Do not hardcode placeholder domains such as `https://ibm.com` as an API endpoint or assume that a particular model ID remains available.

### F. Open-source concepts to adapt carefully

- **OpenEMR-inspired:** configurable, form-driven encounter documentation and mature practice workflows.
- **Bahmni-inspired:** keep modules and state independent enough that a billing UI error does not erase clinical note drafts. Begin with a modular monolith, not a collection of microservices.
- **Medplum-inspired:** API-first domain concepts and a clean, chronological patient timeline.

Adapt the architectural ideas and interaction patterns; do not copy source code or assets without reviewing each project's licence and attribution obligations. Evaluate OpenEMR as a possible ready-made product separately, noting that its own stack/licensing and South African integration fit may differ from this app.

## 4. Architecture and repository approach

Prefer a modular monolith and preserve the existing codebase. Do not introduce microservices, a new database, Kubernetes or Rust unless metrics and a documented use case justify it.

- Frontend: Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, and existing UI library.
- Server/API: existing Node.js backend; if no API framework exists, choose a small established framework that fits the current repository and document the choice.
- Data: existing MongoDB; explicit schemas, indexes, runtime validation, tenant scoping, and atomic/transactional invariants where supported.
- File storage: provider-neutral `DocumentStorage` adapter; local filesystem or mock storage for tests, IBM COS only when configured.
- AI: `AIProvider` interface with `MockAIProvider` and optional `WatsonxAIProvider`.
- Switch: `ClaimsSwitchAdapter` interface with `MockClaimsSwitchAdapter` and future vendor-specific adapters.
- Background jobs: start with a small, replaceable job interface for document processing, notifications, and backup exports. Do not run a CPU-heavy job inside a request/response handler.
- Contracts/validation: shared request/response types and runtime validation.
- Observability: structured logs with request IDs and tenant-safe metadata, metrics and health checks; never dump medical record content into logs.

## 5. Core data model additions

Add only fields supported by tested business requirements. Every tenant-owned entity must be scoped server-side to its practice/tenant.

### Clinical encounter structure (illustrative TypeScript shape)

```ts
type EncounterReviewStatus = "draft" | "ai_draft_ready" | "clinician_reviewed" | "signed";

type EncounterField = {
  fieldId: string;
  sectionKey: string;
  label: string;
  fieldType: "text" | "textarea" | "number" | "date" | "select" | "vitals" | "checkbox";
  value: unknown; // Validate against fieldType/template schema at runtime.
};

type Encounter = {
  id: string;
  tenantId: string;
  patientId: string;
  providerId: string;
  occurredAt: string; // ISO-8601 UTC
  templateId: string;
  templateVersion: number;
  formData: EncounterField[];
  reviewStatus: EncounterReviewStatus;
  aiMetadata?: {
    provider: "mock" | "ibm-watsonx-ai";
    modelId?: string;
    promptVersion: string;
    generatedAt: string;
    sourceDocumentIds: string[];
    reviewStatus: "not_generated" | "pending_review" | "approved" | "rejected";
    approvedBy?: string;
    approvedAt?: string;
  };
};
```

Do not allow arbitrary `unknown` values through to persistence without validating them against the selected template schema. Prefer explicit typed values per field type when implementing production schemas.

Initial entities:

- `Practice`, `Branch`, `User`, `Role`, `Permission`, `ProviderProfile`
- `Patient`, `MedicalAidMembership`, `ConsentRecord`, `Appointment`
- `EncounterTemplate`, `Encounter`, `ClinicalNote`, `Document`, `Prescription`, `Referral`, `Allergy`, `Medication`
- `ServiceCode`, `TariffVersion`, `Invoice`, `InvoiceLine`, `Payment`, `PaymentAllocation`
- `Claim`, `ClaimLine`, `ClaimSubmission`, `SwitchResponse`, `Remittance`
- `AuditEvent`, `Subscription`, `UsageLedger`, `BackupRun`, `AIJob`

Keep internal IDs separate from visible patient numbers. Do not log South African ID numbers or complete medical-aid membership numbers. Encrypt sensitive fields using a reviewed approach where required; an environment-variable string alone does not establish secure key management.

## 7. Backup, environment validation, and deployment requirements

### Backup worker

Implement `src/workers/backup.worker.ts` as a job/command, not as a request handler. Requirements:

- Export only synthetic data in development until production backup is separately approved.
- Use MongoDB's supported backup/export utilities appropriate to the deployment and document prerequisites.
- Write an encrypted backup artifact, validate completion/size/checksum, and upload it to a private COS bucket when configured.
- Use clear names such as `backups/database/backup_YYYY_MM_DDTHHmmssZ.archive.gz`; do not put tenant-specific labels on a whole-database dump that contains multiple tenants.
- If per-tenant exports are required, implement an explicitly filtered, tested export with tenant isolation and reconciliation checks. Never imply that a full database dump contains only one tenant.
- Keep backup credentials separate from application credentials; use least-privilege access.
- Add retention configuration, failure alerts, checksums, backup audit events, and a restore-test script. A backup is not considered successful until a restore test has been completed on a safe environment.
- Do not claim encryption because an archive is merely gzip-compressed. Use a reviewed authenticated-encryption/key-management design; do not commit or log keys.
- Schedule through a documented, configured scheduler/job mechanism; do not assume a free service can run a permanent always-on worker.

### Environment validation

Create `src/utils/env-validator.ts` (or the project's equivalent) to validate required environment variables and fail fast on missing production settings. Validate names, endpoint formats and expected formats without logging their values. Use separate development/test/production modes. Do not require `WATSONX_API_KEY` when AI is configured to `mock`, and do not stop a non-AI workflow from starting when optional AI credentials are intentionally absent.

Example `.env.example` placeholders:


Use the exact variable names expected by the existing application. Do not put placeholders that look like real credentials or pretend that `DATABASE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` alone guarantees POPIA compliance. Load server-only values only on the server; never prefix secrets with `NEXT_PUBLIC_`.

### Docker and Code Engine

Build a multi-stage Docker image using the repository's pinned Node.js version and package manager lockfile. Run as a non-root user, install only production dependencies in the runtime image, include health checks, and do not copy `.env` or test patient data into the image. Add a deployment README describing image build, registry push, Code Engine deployment, environment configuration, scale-to-zero settings, health checks, rollback, and monitoring. Test the container locally before deploying.

### Automated tests

- Unit tests for form schema validation, AI approval states, invoice money calculations and claim lifecycle transitions.
- Tenant-isolation tests for patients, documents, timeline, invoices, claims, export endpoints and background jobs.
- Integration tests for mock AI, mock switch, object-storage adapter and backup failure paths.
- API tests for authentication, authorisation, invalid inputs, rate limits and idempotency.
- End-to-end tests for a full synthetic workflow: register patient -> book appointment -> create encounter -> generate AI draft -> clinician review -> create invoice -> validate mock claim -> show result on timeline.
- CI tasks: lint, typecheck, tests, dependency/security checks and build.
- Add negative tests. A successful happy path is not sufficient for a medical record or billing workflow.

## 8. South African privacy and operational readiness

Before a live pilot, obtain appropriate legal, privacy and security review for POPIA, special personal information, lawful processing, notices/consent where required, operator agreements, retention/deletion, cross-border processing, data-subject requests, incident and breach handling, and Information Officer responsibilities. Also review relevant HPCSA or other professional rules for the user types supported.

Test and document:

- Role-based access, least privilege, privileged-user MFA, session security and secure password/reset flows.
- Tenant isolation enforced by the server, plus audit logs for record reads/changes and exports.
- Encryption in transit and at rest, key rotation/access controls, document permissions, upload limits, malware scanning before production and safe downloads.
- Data location, subprocessors, AI terms, provider retention/training settings, contract terms and transfer mechanisms for every external service.
- Clinical record versioning, signed note corrections, consent, minimum-necessary disclosure and data retention.
- Database/object storage backup, restore procedure, recovery objectives, incident response, vulnerability/dependency scanning, monitoring and penetration testing.
- Explicit user disclosure and review for any clinical decision-support or generated clinical content.

This is a technical checklist, not legal advice or a certification that the product complies with South African law.
